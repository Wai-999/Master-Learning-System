Causal building blocks

Five ideas used throughout this page. The abstract pattern is on the left of each card; how it shows up in your dataset is below the line.

Chain junction

Mediation

A influences B, which in turn influences C. B transmits A's effect — it's a real pathway, not a coincidence.

In this dataset: Fed Policy Stance → DXY → Gold_Price. Tighter policy strengthens the dollar, and a stronger dollar mechanically makes USD-priced gold more expensive worldwide.
Fork junction

Confounding

B independently causes both A and C. A and C will look correlated with each other — but that's because of B, not because one causes the other.

In this dataset: a Risk-Off Shock drives both VIXCLS and Gold_Price up together. VIX doesn't cause gold to rise; both react to the same shock.
Collider junction

Selection bias

A and B independently cause C. They may be totally unrelated — but if you filter or condition your analysis on C, you can manufacture a fake link between A and B.

In this dataset: Fed Policy and a Risk-Off Shock both act on Gold_Price. Slicing your data to "days Gold moved >2%" to study the link between rate surprises and geopolitics can invent a correlation that isn't really there.
Bayesian network

Wiring it all together

Chain every fork, chain, and collider together across all variables and you get a directed acyclic graph (DAG) — add a probability table at each node and it's a Bayesian network, which supports formal queries like "P(Gold up | DXY down, VIX up)".

Tools: networkx to draw it, pgmpy / bnlearn to fit conditional probability tables and query it, causalnex / dowhy for causal-effect estimation.
Cause & effect

Correlation, prediction, causation

Predictive (Granger)

"X helps predict Y" — X's past values reduce forecast error for Y. Easy to test, but silent on why, and blind to shared confounders.

Structural (interventional)

"If we intervened on X, Y would change" — the actual claim gold traders care about. Requires a causal model or a natural experiment to identify.

A famous way to get a structural answer in macro-finance: use narrow, high-frequency windows around FOMC surprises, where almost nothing else could plausibly be moving markets at that instant.

Further reading: Granger (1969) for the predictive definition; Kuttner (2001) for the FOMC-surprise identification approach; Pearl & Mackenzie (2018) for why the distinction matters.

About the *_is_filled columns

Every FRED-sourced indicator above (ANFCI, CPIAUCSL, DFII10, DGS10, DGS2, FEDFUNDS, M2SL, NFCI, RECPROUSM156N, STLFSI4, T10Y2Y, T10YIE, VIXCLS) has a companion _is_filled column. A filled value is carried forward from the last real release — common for monthly series (CPIAUCSL, M2SL, FEDFUNDS, RECPROUSM156N) or weekly series (NFCI, ANFCI, STLFSI4) mapped onto a daily grid — it is not a new observation. Never assume a forward-filled value was known earlier than its true release date (see Stage 3 of the Framework), and consider adding "days since last real update" as its own feature.

Gold technical / autoregressive indicators

Computed purely from Gold_Price's own history — valuable for short-horizon momentum and mean-reversion signal, but not "causes" of gold in the real-world sense above. Must be lagged to the forecast origin or they leak the future.
